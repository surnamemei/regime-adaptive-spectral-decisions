# Regime-adaptive branch decisions for deficient-order fits of spectral clusters

Jinghang Mei

## Abstract

Near a representation exchange, noise can change which branch a deficient-order spectral fit selects. We develop a rule that declares the branch preferred by the noiseless signal or returns an inconclusive decision. The model consists of a positive tone cluster and a phase-opposed remote tone at known frequency, observed with a known phase reference and circular Gaussian noise. A linear contrast test is calibrated when noise dominates the finite-spacing threshold displacement; a restricted fitting-cost test is calibrated when noise is small relative to the cluster defect. A data-dependent selector combines their complementary validity regimes. Under a nondegenerate two-branch exchange condition, unconditional wrong-declaration probability is asymptotically bounded by the nominal level as cluster spacing tends to zero, uniformly over remote amplitudes near the limiting threshold, bounded noise-to-defect ratios and compact positive cluster classes. An exact selection identity and anti-concentration of an independent noise estimate control the overlap. At vanishing noise-to-defect ratio, a margin of many noise widths suffices for consistent correct declarations. Operating curves show gains over cost-only testing at critical noise. Compared with the agreement rule, gains at critical noise accompany losses at weak noise.

## Keywords

line spectral estimation; model order mismatch; closely spaced frequencies; nonlinear least squares; hypothesis testing; uniform inference
